In [Recreating NACA Airfoils](/blog/naca-airfoils), I worked with airfoil shapes in CAD. That left me with a question I could actually test: if I cut a NACA 2412 out of foam and 3D-print another from the same profile, will the air follow both surfaces the same way?

## From CAD to the printer

I created the wind tunnel CAD models in SolidWorks and 3D-printed the first sections at 50% scale. One detail that matters at that size is the fan mount. The printed opening shrinks with the model, but the fan I buy has fixed dimensions. I'm working through the fit and orientation so the fan draws air through the test section and out through the widening diffuser.

One piece I've already printed is the smoke struts which will allow smoke to enter the test chamber. I want to be able to watch a thin streak of smoke pass over the wing and see where it starts to pull away from the upper surface. Before putting either wing in, I plan to run the tunnel empty. If a streak already bends or wanders, I'll want to know that before attributing it to the airfoil.

## The first comparison

I'll mount the foam and printed wings one at a time, keeping the chord, mounting position, and fan setting the same. At each angle of attack, I'll photograph the smoke from the same side of the test section. The detail I'm most curious about is the leading edge: how closely can I reproduce that curve in foam, and can I see a difference in the flow as I tilt the wing?

I’ve printed most of the tunnel parts. Once I finish printing the flow straightener, I’ll assemble the sections with screws and PLA glue. Then I can run smoke through the empty test section to see how straight the flow is before mounting either airfoil.
